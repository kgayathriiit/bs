    A simplified teaching model, not the full Simulink model. Voltage = 1 − 0.08 × battery loading and frequency = 50 − 0.5 × battery loading (droop style). Start-up jolts (cold-load pickup) fade with a 0.5 s time constant. The battery trips above 125 % at once, or above 100 % for 1 s. One second of game time uses one minute of battery and diesel.

    The town’s grid is about to fail.

    When it does, every building goes dark. Only the big battery can start the power again, and it can give only 400 kW. Switch on too much at once and it trips.

    Your job: bring all five buildings back as fast as you can. Solar and diesel can help, if you switch them on.

        What you just did is called black start

        After a total blackout, a microgrid cannot wait for the main grid. A battery forms the voltage first, and every load and source is brought back one step at a time, in an order that keeps voltage and frequency inside safe limits.

        Our research at the SPEL Lab, IIT Gandhinagar, lets an algorithm choose that order automatically: the most load back in the least time, using the most solar and the least diesel.
